On **17 August 2026**, applicants began queueing outside the exam centre in
Kathmandu at midday. They waited through the afternoon, through the night, and
into the following day.

We are told roughly **1,200 people were in that queue. There were 187 places.**

That is about six people for every seat — and this was registration for **A2**,
the second-lowest level of the six. One applicant who queued the full
twenty-four hours, sleeping outside, secured a place for an exam nine days
later.

## What this means

It means the usual advice — *study hard, then take the exam* — describes a
process that does not exist. The real sequence is: study hard, then win a
lottery you enter by standing in the street overnight.

It also means a number of things that follow, and which nobody tells you:

**Your exam date is not under your control.** You can be fully prepared and
still be four months from a certificate.

**The shortage does not stop at A2 — it gets worse.** At B1 and B2 registration
moves online, so you are not sleeping outside; you are refreshing a Facebook page
instead. But the waiting list is long. One applicant on the B1 list in August
2026 was past position **1,000**.

**And B2 appears to run only once a year.** We are told by candidates that after
the July 2026 sitting, the next B2 exam date at the Kathmandu centre is July
2027. If that is right, someone who finishes a B2 course in the summer waits
twelve months for a chance to certify it. We have asked the centre to confirm.

**And where the queue is physical, that is not a neutral cost.** Waiting a full
night outside is not equally available to everybody. It is harder if you are
travelling in from outside the valley, if you have work or family duties, and it
carries real safety risk — particularly for young women queueing alone
overnight.

<div class="warn">

**This explains the B1 wall, and it is not what people say it is.** Teachers in
Nepal report that many students stop at B1 and few go on to B2. That is usually
described as students losing interest.

It is not. If B2 runs once a year, then finishing a B2 course and sitting a B2
exam are separated by up to twelve months — regardless of how good you are or how
hard you work. People are not stopping at B1. **They are being stopped, by a
calendar.** B1 is enough for the visa, and waiting a year to prove B2 is a cost
most people cannot carry.

Someone who sleeps on a pavement for twenty-four hours to sit A2 is not short of
motivation.

</div>

## The rules are different at different levels — and this matters

The Goethe-Zentrum Kathmandu is the Goethe-Institut's cooperating institution in
Nepal, offering certificates at A1 through C1. Its own published information sets
out two quite different registration systems:

**A1 and A2: registration is physical.** Places are given out in person on
announced dates, first come first served. That is why the queue above formed —
it was A2 registration, and standing in it overnight was the process.

**B1 and B2: registration is online.** Applicants submit a form published on the
Goethe-Zentrum Kathmandu **Facebook page**, again first come first served.

If you are heading for an Ausbildung, B1 is your level — so **watch that Facebook
page**, not the street. The form appearing there is the event you cannot afford
to miss.

The centre states it can take a **maximum of 70 applicants for exams at all
levels**, across **three Sundays a month**. That is around 200 places a
registration round, which fits the 187 reported above.

And one rule matters more than the seat count: **B1 and B2 registration reopens
only once every applicant who already submitted a form has been given a date.**
Registration is not a monthly event you can plan around. It reopens when the
queue clears — which means the wait is open-ended rather than merely long.

## What to do about it

**Plan the exam first, and everything else around it.** Most guides treat the
certificate as step one of many. On this evidence it is the longest and least
predictable step. Work backwards from the German training year: chamber guidance
is 4–6 months from contract to entry, and your employer needs the certificate
before the contract.

**For B1 and B2, follow the Goethe-Zentrum Kathmandu Facebook page.** That is
where the registration form is published, and allocation is first come first
served. The day you submit matters more than how ready you feel that week.

**For A1 and A2, expect to queue in person.** People go in groups and take turns.
If you have to be there overnight, be there with someone — that is a safety
matter, not just a comfort one.

**Three institutions are accepted, not one — and you can mix them.**

Read this next part carefully. It is the most useful thing on this site and
getting it wrong costs money and months.

The German Embassy in Kathmandu accepts certificates from three providers, and
explicitly allows you to sit **different parts of the exam with different
providers**. Its own words:

> Es ist möglich einzelne Prüfungsteile bei verschiedenen anerkannten Anbietern
> zu absolvieren (z.B. 3 Teile beim Goethe Zentrum und 1 Teil bei der VHS). Nur
> Sprachzertifikate der folgenden Institutionen können derzeit anerkannt werden:
> Goethe-Zentrum Kathmandu, VHS (Bhaktapur & Pokhara) oder Telc.

So the accepted list is:

<div class="scroll">

| Provider | Where | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Goethe-Zentrum Kathmandu** | Thapathali | The queue everybody is in |
| **VHS** | **Bhaktapur and Pokhara** | A licensed ÖSD exam centre; also has a Kathmandu branch at Putalisadak |
| **telc** | no centre in Nepal | Accepted, but you would have to sit it abroad |

</div>

<div class="warn">

**Here is what follows, and hardly anyone seems to know any of it.**

**VHS is a second accepted route, and it is in Pokhara as well as Bhaktapur.** If
you are stuck deep in the Goethe waiting list, this is the alternative — and for
anyone outside the Kathmandu valley, Pokhara may be far easier to reach. VHS is
a licensed ÖSD examination centre and takes external candidates, not only its own
students.

**An official ÖSD certificate counts — including from a centre the embassy's list
does not name.** ÖSD keeps a public register of the centres it licenses. For
Nepal it lists three: VHS Bhaktapur, VHS Pokhara, and a third centre in
Kathmandu. In August 2026 we asked the embassy directly whether a certificate
from that third centre would be accepted. The answer: *"Ja, auch diese wird
akzeptiert wenn es sich um ein offizielles ÖSD Zertifikat handelt."* — *"Yes,
that one is accepted too, as long as it is an official ÖSD certificate."* If you
are stuck in a queue, that is a third door in Kathmandu.

**But check three things before you pay anybody.** First, the embassy's published
page still names only the Goethe-Zentrum, VHS and telc, and does not mention ÖSD
anywhere — so the sentence above is the embassy in writing to us, not something
you can point to on their website. Print this page and be ready to ask. Second,
we asked about a centre **in Nepal**; we did not ask about ÖSD sat in another
country, so do not assume that. Third, anyone can call themselves an exam centre,
so check the name against
[ÖSD's own register](https://www.osd.at/en/exam-centres/) — it is free, and it is
the authority on who is licensed.

**You do not need to buy a course to sit the exam — but ask.** ÖSD exams are
course-independent. However, a centre can be licensed to examine only its own
course students rather than outsiders, so if a course and an exam seat are being
sold to you together, ask two questions and get the answers in writing: **can I
sit the exam without taking your course, and what does the exam cost on its
own?** VHS takes external candidates.

**You do not have to sit all four parts in the same place.** The embassy gives
its own example: three parts at the Goethe-Zentrum and one at the VHS. If you can
get some modules but not others, that is not a dead end — it is the documented
route.

**telc is accepted but has no centre in Nepal, and none is planned.** We asked
telc directly in August 2026. Their answer: *"Aktuell bieten wir in Nepal keine
telc-Prüfungen an und es gibt derzeit auch keine konkreten Pläne, ein
Prüfungszentrum dort einzurichten."* — *"At present we do not offer telc
examinations in Nepal, and there are currently no concrete plans to set up an
examination centre there."* They pointed to neighbouring countries instead. So
telc means India — see below.

</div>

## Sitting telc in India — the option nobody mentions

This is worth setting out properly, because it is a real route and the pieces fit
together better than they look.

- **telc is on the embassy's accepted list.** A telc certificate counts for the
  Ausbildung visa.
- **There is no telc centre in Nepal**, and telc told us there are no plans for
  one. The nearest centres are in India, including New Delhi.
- **Nepali citizens can enter India without a visa**, under the 1950 Treaty of
  Peace and Friendship. No visa application, no waiting list, no fee.
- **Group arrangements are possible.** We asked telc whether a group from Nepal
  could get exam places together. Their answer: *"grundsätzlich wäre dies
  möglich, allerdings ist eine direkte Anmeldung bei uns nicht möglich. Die
  lizenzierten telc Prüfungszentren organisieren die telc Prüfungen eigenständig
  und legen die Prüfungstermine fest."* — *"In principle this would be possible,
  but you cannot register directly with us. The licensed telc examination
  centres organise the telc examinations themselves and set the examination
  dates."*

In plain terms: **yes in principle, but you arrange it with an individual
licensed exam centre, not with telc head office.** telc publishes a centre finder
on its website; a centre in Delhi or elsewhere in northern India is the place to
ask about dates and about places for a group.

<div class="warn">

**Whether this is worth it is a calculation, not a principle.** Against it:
roughly a thousand kilometres each way from Kathmandu, travel and accommodation
costs, and telc is not the exam format Nepali courses prepare you for.

For it: if you are deep in a waiting list with no date, a certificate you can
actually obtain in a few weeks may be worth a difficult journey. Several people
travelling together shares the cost, and is safer than going alone.

Work out your own numbers. But do not let anyone tell you it is impossible — it
is not, and the embassy accepts the result.

</div>

**And the certificate must be in the file when you apply.** The embassy states
the language proof *"bereits mit der Antragstellung eingereicht werden muss"* —
submitted with the application itself, not handed in later. So the exam has to be
done before you start the visa process, not alongside it.

## What we still do not know

- Whether B2 really runs only once a year, and if so why
- The realistic wait from submitting the B1/B2 form to actually sitting the exam
- How quickly the online B1/B2 forms fill once published
- Whether the centre's capacity is increasing
- Whether any second provider is likely to be licensed in Nepal

If you find any of this out, it is worth telling other people. This page exists
because one person was willing to say what actually happened to her.
