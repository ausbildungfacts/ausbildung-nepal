An **Ausbildung** is paid vocational training in Germany. You work in a company
and attend a vocational school (*Berufsschule*) at the same time, usually for
three years, and you are paid a wage throughout. At the end you hold a
state-recognised qualification.

It is a real route and thousands of people take it every year. It is also slower
and harder than most agencies will tell you, and some of what you may have been
told is simply not true.

This site exists because the information you can find is mostly written by
people who are paid if you go. Everything here is sourced, and where something
is uncertain it says so.

## Five things worth knowing before you spend any money

**Know what you are paying for.** German lessons and exam fees are real costs
and worth paying. But no one on the German side may charge you for finding your
place — German law puts that cost on the employer. And Nepali institutes are
themselves regulated: they need a licence, must publish their fees, and may not
take cash. People are being quoted 7 to 16 lakh. Ask what the money buys before
you borrow against your family's land to pay it.
[Read the detail](/no-fees/)

**B1 opens the door. B2 is what makes the first year easier.** The embassy in
Kathmandu generally asks for B1, certified and not older than six months, and
you can start applying for training places at B1. Some vocational schools accept
B1; others ask for B2. Plenty of people arrive with B1 and manage. But the
difference shows once you are in Germany and following lessons in German all
day, and teachers in Nepal report that many students stop at B1 and
comparatively few carry on to B2. If you can reach B2 before you fly, do.

**The exam queue is the real bottleneck, not your German.** In August 2026
around 1,200 people queued overnight in Kathmandu for 187 exam places — at A2.
Plan the certificate first and everything else around it.
[Read the detail](/exam-slots/)

**There is a way past the visa waiting list, and your employer holds it.** Since
1 December 2025 the embassy in Kathmandu allocates visa appointments from a
waiting list. But it has confirmed to us that anyone holding a *Vorabzustimmung*
gets an appointment **within three weeks** — email it in and they schedule you.
Only your employer can obtain one. They pay €411 and most have never heard of it.
This is the most useful page on the site. [Read the detail](/visa/)

**For health and care work, the restriction is on agencies — not on you.** Nepal
is on the World Health Organization's health workforce safeguards list. Because
of that, private agencies are prohibited from recruiting or placing people from
Nepal into German health and care jobs, and advertising for it is prohibited
too. Only the Federal Employment Agency may do that placement.

None of that stops **you** applying on your own initiative. What it does mean is
that if an agency approaches you offering nursing work in Germany, the approach
itself is the thing that is not permitted — and that tells you something about
who you are dealing with. If care work is what you want, [the Au Pair
route](/au-pair/) is how several Nepali women have actually got there.

## The two questions people ask us most

**"How do I actually find a place?"** You find it yourself, free. On the day this
site was last checked the German employment agency was advertising 165,002
training places, open to anyone with a browser. Places are not the scarce thing.
An employer willing to take someone who still needs a visa is.
[Where to search, and what to send](/apply/)

**"Can my husband, wife or children come with me?"** There is a route in the
statute for a spouse and for children — but it turns on a condition decided
before you ever leave Nepal, and on whether a trainee's wage supports two people.
Anyone giving you a confident timescale is guessing.
[What the law actually says](/family/)

## What this site will not do

It will not find you a place, take your documents, or ask you for money. It is
not an agency and has nothing to sell.
