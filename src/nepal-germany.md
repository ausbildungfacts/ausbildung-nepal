Most of what reaches you is either a paid service or a rumour. In between there
are real programmes, run by German public bodies, that a person in Nepal can
apply to. They are less famous because there is no commission in them.

None of these is a shortcut. Every one still needs German, and every one still
goes through the same embassy queue. But they are real, and they are free.

## The volunteer year — the route almost nobody mentions

Germany runs two national volunteer services. You work full time for a year at a
social organisation — a care home, a hospital, a kindergarten, a disability
service, an environmental project — and you are given an allowance, usually
accommodation and meals, and insurance.

<div class="scroll">

| | **FSJ** *(Freiwilliges Soziales Jahr)* | **BFD** *(Bundesfreiwilligendienst)* |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Age | Up to your **27th birthday** | **No upper age limit** |
| Length | Usually 12 months, up to 18 | Usually 12 months, up to 18 |
| Repeatable | Once only | Every five years |

</div>

**The embassy in Kathmandu already issues these visas.** *Freiwilligendienste* is
one of the national visa categories it lists — the same page that covers
Ausbildung and family reunification. This is not a theoretical route.

**What you get.** An allowance the law caps at **€676 a month** (the 2026 upper
limit, shared across FSJ, BFD and FÖJ). The actual figure is set by each
organisation and is often lower — one national provider states at least €480 a
month. On top of that, accommodation, meals and work clothing are frequently
provided free or paid for separately. Some providers contribute around €300
towards your journey home after twelve months.

**What it costs you.** Your own flight, and your own time. No agency fee is
involved, and none should be asked for.

**What it needs.** German, in practice at **B1 with a certificate** — providers
recommend it plainly, because you will be working with people who speak no
English. You apply **in German**, sending a CV, your language certificate, your
last school report, a motivation letter and the provider's application form.
[The templates on this site work for this too](/templates/).

There is one extra step people do not expect: the federal office, the **BAFzA**,
has to approve the volunteer agreement before you can get a visa, and that takes
**at least six weeks** on its own. Add it to your plan.

<div class="warn">

**Be honest with yourself about the queue.** The embassy publishes an approximate
**8 to 12 month** wait for Au-Pair and FSJ appointments — the longest it
publishes. A volunteer year is a real route, but it is not the fast one.
[How the waiting list works](/visa/).

</div>

**Why it is interesting anyway.** It is the same shape as [the Au Pair
route](/au-pair/): arrive legally, live in German for a year, get your German up
to where an Ausbildung actually needs it, and be in the country when you apply.
Whether a volunteer permit can then be changed into an Ausbildung permit is a
question for the local Ausländerbehörde, and **we have not confirmed it** — ask
before you build a plan on it.

## weltwärts — funded by a German ministry, and Nepal is in it

**weltwärts** is a volunteer programme run jointly by Germany's Federal Ministry
for Economic Cooperation and Development (**BMZ**) and civil society
organisations. Most people know the version that sends young Germans abroad. The
part that matters here is the **South–North component**, which brings young
people from partner countries *to* Germany for six to eighteen months.

**Nepal is on the list of partner countries.** Eligibility runs off the OECD DAC
list, and the programme describes itself as *"a programme for everyone —
regardless of budget"*. It is publicly funded; it is not something you buy.

**The catch is how you get in.** You cannot apply to weltwärts directly, and you
cannot apply from outside its network. Volunteers are **nominated by a partner
organisation in their own country**, and each German host works with its own
specific partners — the programme itself notes that not every DAC-listed country
has an active sending organisation.

So the practical question is not "how do I apply to weltwärts" but **"which
organisations in Nepal are weltwärts partners"**. That is worth asking your
German teacher, and worth asking any NGO you already have a connection to. If
you find one, tell us and it will go on this page.

## NSST — worth investigating, and we could not verify it

The **Nepal Secretariat of Skills and Training** describes itself as a
Nepal–German skills initiative, set up in 2021 as a German non-profit company
(*gGmbH*), working on dual vocational training between the two countries and
matching Nepali students with German companies for apprenticeships. Its public
material states that **its services are free of charge to students**, including
preparation and the matching itself, with mentoring during the Ausbildung.

<div class="warn">

**We could not check this at source.** Their own website did not respond on any
of the days we tried — re-checked on 23 August 2026, when one address failed to
load at all and the other returned a gateway timeout — so everything above comes
from their public profiles rather than from their own pages. We have written to
ask them directly, and this section will change if they answer. **We are not recommending them and we are not warning
against them** — we are telling you they exist and that we could not verify it.

Before you hand anything over, ask the five questions on [the fees
page](/no-fees/), and these two as well: *what exactly is free, and what is not?*
and *which German companies have you actually placed Nepali trainees with?* An
organisation that is what it says it is will answer both without difficulty.

</div>

## Au Pair

Covered on its own page, because it comes up often as an alternative to
Ausbildung — usually when the exam queue looks impossible, which is the wrong
reason to choose it. It works, it is slow, and the visa queue is the longest of
all. [Read it before you choose it](/au-pair/).

## What none of these do

**None of them removes the German requirement.** Every route on this page needs
real German, and most want a certificate. The Federal Employment Agency put it
plainly when we asked about the government route: home-country language learning
is *"das zentrale Nadelöhr"*, the central bottleneck, for any successful start.

**None of them is an Ausbildung.** A volunteer year is a volunteer year. It can
put you in Germany, in German, legally — which is worth a great deal — but it
does not end in a state-recognised qualification the way [an
Ausbildung](/apply/) does.

**None of them skips the queue.** They all go through the same embassy in
Kathmandu, and for FSJ that queue is the longest one published.

## A footnote: the government declaration people keep asking about

Nepal and Germany signed a **joint declaration on skilled labour migration**,
reported to involve a year of paid vocational training, employment afterwards,
online applications, and workers paying nothing. Nepal signed something
comparable with Romania around the same time.

We asked the Bundesagentur für Arbeit, which signed it for Germany. Their reply,
August 2026:

> Die gemeinsame Absichtserklärung zwischen Deutschland und Nepal bildet das
> politische Fundament für eine vertiefte Zusammenarbeit in der
> Fachkräftemigration, ein offenes oder direktes Bewerbungsverfahren für
> Einzelpersonen über die ZAV existiert derzeit jedoch nicht.

It is **a political foundation, not a programme you can join.** There is no open
or direct application procedure for individuals. When such partnerships do run,
they run as cohort-based pilot projects through official partner structures in
Nepal — often with GIZ and the Nepali labour ministry — *"und nicht über eine
freie Initiativbewerbung bei der Bundesagentur für Arbeit"*.

<div class="warn">

**So: there is nothing to apply for.** Do not wait for it, and do not let anyone
put your name on a list for it. **If someone offers to register you for a
government programme between Nepal and Germany, they are selling something that
does not currently exist.**

</div>

Two things that get mixed up with it. **"Free visa, free ticket"** is a Nepali
policy covering seven Gulf destinations — it has never covered Germany or the EU,
and Nepal's Labour Minister announced scrapping it in February 2026. And the
ministry that negotiated the declaration, MoLESS, was **dissolved on 13 May
2026**, its labour functions moving to the new Ministry of Youth, Labour and
Employment — so older articles and anyone still pointing you at MoLESS are out of
date.
