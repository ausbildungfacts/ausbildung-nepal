Here is the situation to avoid. You apply, you are accepted, you sign a training
contract. Then the visa takes longer than anyone expected, and by the time it
arrives the training year has started without you.

Vocational school runs on an annual cycle. A visa that comes in October does not
cost you a few weeks — it can cost you the whole year, with the contract already
signed.

This is avoidable, and mostly by things agreed **in advance**.

## Three things to ask for before you sign

**1. Ask for the contract to take effect when the visa is issued.**

German chambers themselves advise employers to write the contract so that it
only becomes effective once the visa has been granted. With that clause, a delay
postpones your start. Without it, a delay can end the agreement.

You can ask for this plainly:

> *Könnten wir in den Vertrag aufnehmen, dass er wirksam wird, sobald das Visum
> erteilt ist?*

**2. Ask for a realistic start date.**

German chamber guidance for employers is to allow **4 to 6 months** between
signing the contract and entry, and to recruit **6 to 8 months** before the
intended start. From Nepal, with the current appointment waiting list, treat
those as minimums rather than estimates.

If your employer is planning on six weeks, they have not done this before. That
is not a reason to walk away — it is a reason to tell them early, while the date
can still be moved.

**3. Make sure they know about the accelerated procedure.**

Most small German companies have never heard of § 81a. It is the one lever that
can genuinely shorten the wait, **only the employer can start it**, and it costs
them €411. Send them [the page about it](/visa/) before you sign, not when you
are already stuck.

## If the date is already a problem

**The start date can be moved.** Before the training begins, employer and
trainee can sign an amendment — an *Änderungsvereinbarung* — setting a new start
date. The end date moves with it. Training does not have to begin in September:
chambers hold two examination sittings a year and assign candidates by their
contract end date.

**The contract cannot be backdated.** That is not permitted, so there is no
paperwork solution that pretends you arrived earlier than you did.

**Talk to the chamber.** The Handwerkskammer or IHK that registered your contract
handles these amendments routinely. Your employer contacts them, not you — but
your employer may not know it is possible, and you now do.

<div class="warn">

**Raise it early, even if it feels awkward.** People in your position often stay
quiet, worried that asking questions makes them look difficult before they have
even started. It does the opposite. An employer who hears in March that entry
takes four to six months can plan around it. The same employer hearing it in
August has lost a training place and a year of planning, and will remember who
did not mention it.

</div>
